How SOL uses AI

Last updated: 2026-10-31

The short version

  • SOL's plans and replies are written by Claude, an AI made by Anthropic, a company in the United States.
  • To write them, SOL sends Anthropic what each job needs: your answers, logs, chats and recent Apple Health data.
  • Anthropic doesn't train its AI on your data and deletes it within 30 days.
  • Your account and everything SOL writes for you is stored in Sydney, Australia.
  • You can turn AI off anytime in Settings. SOL can't work without it, so that deletes your SOL account.

What SOL uses AI for

SOL's daily briefs, weekly plans, monthly reports, chat replies and food estimates are all written by Claude. That's what makes SOL a coach rather than a tracker: it reads your own numbers and writes advice for you.

Where your data goes

Here is exactly what happens when SOL writes something for you.

1. Your phone sends your data to SOL's server in Sydney

SOL's server and database run on Amazon Web Services in Sydney, Australia. That's where your account, answers, logs and Apple Health numbers are stored.

2. SOL's server sends Anthropic what that job needs

Anthropic, in the United States, receives the request, and Claude writes the plan, brief or reply.

3. Claude's reply comes back and is stored in Sydney

Your plan, brief or reply is saved in your SOL account in Sydney, not at Anthropic.

What's sent, and what isn't

Depending on the job, SOL can send Anthropic:

  • your name and setup answers, including anything you wrote about conditions, injuries or medications, word for word;
  • your goals, plan, training history and logs, and your notes, check-ins and pain entries;
  • recent Apple Health numbers (daily values for up to the last 28 days), such as sleep, heart rate, heart rate variability, weight, workouts, nutrition and, if you track it, your cycle, plus the scores and trends SOL works out from them;
  • your chat messages;
  • the short list of things SOL remembers about you (see What the AI remembers);
  • SOL's own earlier plans and briefs, so its advice stays consistent;
  • for the monthly report, the text of lab results or scans you add;
  • if you train together with someone, the goal's name and its owner's display name.

What isn't sent:

  • Your voice. If you log by voice, your speech is turned into text on your phone. The audio is never saved and never leaves your phone. Only the text, after you've seen it, is sent.
  • More than the job needs. Food logging sends only the text of what you ate.

What Anthropic does with it

  • It doesn't train its AI on your data. Its contract with SOL forbids it.
  • It deletes it within 30 days. That covers both what SOL sends and Claude's reply.
  • If Anthropic's automatic safety checks flag a request as possibly breaking its rules, it can keep that request for up to 2 years (and the related safety scores for up to 7 years).
  • It works for SOL under a data processing agreement, and only to provide the service.

What the AI remembers

Claude itself remembers nothing between requests. It starts fresh every time, and SOL sends what's needed with each request. (Anthropic keeps a short cache for about 5 minutes so repeated requests are faster.)

The memory lives in your SOL account in Sydney. Along with your plans and logs, SOL keeps a short list of things you've told it in chat so it can coach you better, like "prefers evening sessions" or "walks the dog in the mornings". It holds up to 30 short items. SOL picks them from your messages, skips passing comments and anything already logged elsewhere (such as pain, goals, training and food), and tidies the list once a week, merging repeats and dropping things whose time has passed. The list is sent to Claude with each plan, brief, report and chat reply.

You can see the list and delete any item, or all of it, in Settings, What SOL remembers. Deleting your account deletes it too.

Sending your data overseas

Anthropic stores what SOL sends in the United States, and may process a request in other countries where it runs its AI. SOL can't tell you in advance which country that will be.

It's worth knowing that privacy law in those countries isn't the same as New Zealand's, and may not protect your data in the same way. So the protection your data gets at Anthropic comes mainly from SOL's contract with Anthropic, rather than from the law there. When SOL asks whether you're happy for it to use AI, choosing "Sounds good" means you agree to this.

Who at SOL sees your data

No one at SOL looks at your data as a matter of course. Ruby, who runs SOL, can access the database only to keep the service running or fix a problem, and reads a report only if someone reports you.

Turning AI off

SOL can't work without AI, so turning AI off means deleting your SOL account. In Settings, go to AI and tap Turn off AI, then confirm. SOL stops sending anything to Anthropic and deletes your account and everything in it. If you cancel, nothing changes. Anything Anthropic already has is deleted within 30 days, or up to 2 years if it was flagged by a safety check.
